# VERIFIED MALAYSIA & GLOBAL FACT BANK (researched Nov 2026 — use these, do not invent others)

## Regulators & frameworks
- Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) regulates banks, payment systems, e-money, money services; Securities Commission Malaysia (SC) regulates capital market fintech: P2P financing, equity crowdfunding (ECF), digital asset exchanges (DAX), digital investment management (DIM/robo-advisory), IEO operators.
- BNM issued the **Licensing Framework for Digital Banks** in December 2020; digital banks face an asset limit of RM3 billion during a foundational phase of 3–5 years (BNM, 2020).
- BNM **Financial Sector Blueprint 2022–2026** sets five desired outcomes; e-payments CAGR target ~15% (BNM, 2022).
- BNM **Financial Technology Regulatory Sandbox Framework** (revised February 2024).
- BNM **e-KYC policy document** (first 2020; revised April 2024) enabling non-face-to-face onboarding.
- BNM **Risk Management in Technology (RMiT)** policy document (2020; revised 2023; updated November 2025) — technology and cyber risk requirements for financial institutions.
- BNM **Discussion Paper on Artificial Intelligence in the Malaysian Financial Sector** (August 2025).
- BNM **Discussion Paper on Asset Tokenisation in the Malaysian Financial Sector** (October 2025).
- SC **Guidelines on Recognized Markets** regulate P2P/ECF/DAX operators (Recognized Market Operators, RMOs).
- SC **Guidelines on Digital Assets** (issued 28 October 2020; revised 19 August 2024) — cover digital token offerings (IEOs) and IEO operators; issued under s.377 Capital Markets and Services Act 2007.
- **Capital Markets and Services (Prescription of Securities) (Digital Currency and Digital Token) Order 2019** (effective 15 January 2019) — digital currencies/tokens prescribed as securities in Malaysia.
- SC **Digital Investment Management (DIM) framework** introduced May 2017 — first in the region — licensing robo-advisors.
- Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (Act 709), amended 2024. Financial Services Act 2013; Islamic Financial Services Act 2013; Central Bank of Malaysia Act 2009 (makes the Shariah Advisory Council the apex authority for Islamic finance). Anti-Money Laundering, Anti-Terrorism Financing and Proceeds of Unlawful Activities Act 2001 (AMLA).
- Consumer bodies: Ombudsman for Financial Services (OFS); BNM's Fair Treatment of Financial Consumers policy development (exposure draft 2024). MAS (Singapore) FEAT principles (Fairness, Ethics, Accountability, Transparency, 2018) are the regional benchmark for AI ethics.

## Digital banks (licences announced 29 April 2022; five licensees)
- **GXBank** (Grab-led, with Kuok Group): first to begin operations, 1 September 2023.
- **Boost Bank** (Boost/Axiata + RHB Banking Group): commenced 15 January 2024.
- **AEON Bank** (AEON Financial Service, AEON Credit, MoneyLion): launched 26 May 2024 — Malaysia's first Islamic digital bank.
- **KAF Digital Bank** (KAF Investment Bank consortium with Carsome, MoneyMatch, Jirnexu, StoreHub): second Islamic digital bank; approved to commence 20 December 2024.
- **Ryt Bank** (Sea Ltd + YTL Digital Capital): approved to commence 20 December 2024.
- BNM Governor (2025): digital banks in "foundational phase", subject to asset size limitations; deposits growing from underserved/unserved segments; credit products rolling out gradually.

## Payments (PayNet = Payments Network Malaysia, BNM-owned operator)
- PayNet processed **8.44 billion digital payment transactions in 2025** (PayNet, 2026).
- BNM: **18.4 billion e-payment transactions in 2025** (up 25% from 14.7b in 2024); average 538 transactions per Malaysian; retail e-payment value RM831 billion (2025) vs RM698.2b (2024); average e-money transaction RM43 (2025) vs RM33 (2024).
- **DuitNow QR** volume doubled to ~3 billion transactions in 2025 (from 1.5b in 2024); ~3 million merchant touchpoints (2.6m in 2024), many new ones cash-only MSMEs.
- Cross-border DuitNow QR linkages live with **Thailand, Indonesia, Singapore, China**; **Cambodia** added 2025; **India** expected 2026; cross-border QR transactions grew 2.5× in 2025.
- Rails: RENTAS (real-time gross settlement, since 1999), Interbank GIRO (IBG), FPX (online bank-to-bank payments), JomPAY (bill payments, 2015), MyDebit (debit), DuitNow (instant P2P via proxy IDs, 2018), DuitNow QR (national QR standard, 2019).
- Major e-wallets: Touch 'n Go eWallet, MAE by Maybank, GrabPay, Boost, ShopeePay, BigPay.

## P2P financing & equity crowdfunding (SC Annual Report 2025)
- **P2P financing**: cumulative **RM11.32 billion raised via 154,283 campaigns** since inception (2016); **26% of funds Shariah-compliant** (2025). 2025: RM2.83b raised (+13% YoY), 33,913 campaigns; 62% of campaigns raised ≤ RM50,000; 98% for working capital; wholesale & retail trade largest sector (51% of 2025 funds, RM1.44b); total issuers 24,683; >53,000 investors cumulatively; 2025 investors 15,257 (20% new); issuers 99% non-tech; 49% of issuers ≥6 years in business; Selangor+KL = 54% of issuers.
- **ECF**: cumulative **RM917.51 million across 445 campaigns** since inception (2016); Shariah-compliant 4.1%. 2025: RM139.34m (+42.4%), 42 successful campaigns; 45% of 2025 campaigns raised >RM3m; seed & Series A stages dominate; 82% of issuers in Selangor/KL.
- Malaysia was the first ASEAN country to regulate ECF (SC framework 2015) and among the first to regulate P2P (2016).
- Example operators: P2P — Funding Societies, Fundaztic, B2B FinPAL, microLEAP (Shariah-compliant notes), Alixco, CapBay, MoneySave; ECF — pitchIN, Ata Plus, MyStartr, Ethis, Leet Capital.

## Digital investment management (robo-advisory)
- Nine registered DIMs (SC, via Capital Markets Malaysia); examples: StashAway (first licensed, 2018), Wahed Invest (Shariah), MYTHEO (GAX MD), Raiz, Akru, Versa, Kenanga Digital Investing (KDI), BEST Invest (BIMB Investment — Shariah).
- Fund management industry AUM RM1.07 trillion (2024, +9.6% YoY). DIM AUM <1% of industry but growing (15% in 2023); >1 million DIM accounts; 51% of accounts held by investors in their 20s.
- DIM services: automated risk profiling, suitability assessment, asset allocation, rebalancing — minimal human supervision.

## Crypto & digital assets in Malaysia
- Registered DAXs include Luno Malaysia, SINEGY Technologies, Tokenize Technology, Hata Digital (SC list updated regularly).
- SC Shariah Advisory Council resolution (2020): digital assets recognised as *mal* (property) from a Shariah perspective; trading on SC-registered DAXs is permissible (with conditions).
- Crypto is NOT legal tender in Malaysia; regulated as securities via the 2019 Prescription Order.
- Global facts allowed: Bitcoin whitepaper 2008 (Nakamoto), network launched Jan 2009; 21 million cap; April 2024 halving cut block reward to 3.125 BTC; Ethereum launched 2015 (Buterin), switched to proof-of-stake ("The Merge", September 2022); FTX collapse November 2022; ICO boom 2017–2018.

## CBDC
- **Project Dunbar (2021)**: BNM with BIS Innovation Hub, Reserve Bank of Australia, MAS, South African Reserve Bank — prototype multi-CBDC (mCBDC) platform for cross-border settlement.
- Financial Sector Blueprint 2022–2026: multi-year CBDC exploration via proof-of-concept in three phases — wholesale cross-border → full-scale wholesale → retail consideration.
- Project mBridge reached minimum-viable-product stage (2024); BNM participates. BNM has run wholesale digital ringgit trials; digital ringgit PoC report 2025. No decision to issue a retail CBDC; BNM states CBDC would be a central-bank liability, not a crypto-asset, using permissioned ledgers.
- BNM is advancing work on tokenised deposits and ringgit stablecoins (2026 agenda).

## Financial inclusion (World Bank Global Findex)
- Malaysia account ownership (adults 15+): 2011: 66.2%; 2014: 80.7%; 2017: 85.3%; 2021: 88.4%; 2024: 88.7%. Gender gap <1pt (2024). Poorest 40%: 82.0% (2024). (World Bank Global Findex Database 2025 / 2022 report.)

## Islamic & sustainable finance
- **Global Islamic Fintech (GIFT) Report 2025/26** (DinarStandard & Elipses, 2026): Islamic fintech market ≈ US$198b transaction volume (2024/25), projected US$341b by 2029 (~11.5% CAGR); 484 Islamic fintech firms globally; top ecosystems: Saudi Arabia, Malaysia, UAE, Indonesia, Bahrain.
- Malaysia: global leader in Islamic finance via Malaysia International Islamic Financial Centre (MIFC); Value-based Intermediation (VBI) initiative by BNM (2018); BNM Shariah standards (e.g., Murabahah, Tawarruq, Wakalah, Baiʿ al-Sarf).
- Islamic fintech examples: HelloGold (Shariah digital gold savings, est. 2015), Ethis (Islamic P2P/ECF), Global Sadaqah (waqf/sadaqah platform), microLEAP, Wahed Invest, BEST Invest, AEON Bank, KAF Digital Bank; 26% of P2P funds Shariah-compliant (2025).
- Sustainable finance: BNM **Climate Change and Principle-based Taxonomy (CCPT, 2021)**; **Joint Committee on Climate Change (JC3, 2019)** co-chaired by BNM & SC; world's first green sukuk issued by Tadau Energy (Malaysia, 2017); SC Sustainable and Responsible Investment (SRI) framework (2014) & SRI Roadmap (2019); SDG-linked and ESG investing growth.

## Cybersecurity & fraud
- National Scam Response Centre (NSRC, hotline 997, est. 2022) — rapid freezing of scam-linked funds.
- PDRM **SemakMule** portal/app — check mule-account bank numbers/phone numbers.
- PayNet operates fraud intelligence/alert initiatives with banks; BNM directed banks to tighten controls (e.g., kill-switch to freeze accounts, blocking SMS OTP for high-risk transactions, one secured device per account).
- RMiT sets cyber-resilience duties; Cyber Security Act 2024 and NACSA oversee national critical information infrastructure.

## Algorithmic trading / markets
- Bursa Malaysia permits algorithmic/direct market access trading under its rules; retail algo adoption growing.
- Robo-advisors in Malaysia regulated under DIM framework (see above).
- Globally, algorithmic trading executes the majority of equity-market volume in developed markets (textbook-accepted).
