## Preface

Financial services are being rebuilt in real time. A hawker in Kota Bharu now accepts QR payments through DuitNow; a fresh graduate in Johor Bahru invests RM300 a month through a licensed robo-advisor; a micro-enterprise in Kuching raises working capital from hundreds of small investors on a peer-to-peer platform; and five newly licensed digital banks are opening accounts for Malaysians whom conventional banking never reached. Understanding these changes is no longer optional for students of business, banking or technology — it is foundational.

This textbook was written specifically for **Malaysian diploma and undergraduate students**. Global FinTech theory is presented rigorously and cited to the scholarly literature (APA 7th edition), but every concept is anchored to the Malaysian market: Bank Negara Malaysia's (BNM) digital bank licensing framework, the Securities Commission Malaysia's (SC) regulation of P2P financing, equity crowdfunding, digital asset exchanges and robo-advisors, PayNet's national payment rails, and Malaysia's world-leading Islamic finance ecosystem.

### How to Use This Book

Each chapter follows a consistent pedagogical structure designed around Bloom's Taxonomy:

- **Chapter Outline** — the sections you will cover.
- **Learning Outcomes** — six outcomes tagged to Bloom's cognitive levels (Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyse, Evaluate, Create).
- **Opening Vignette** — a realistic Malaysian scenario that motivates the chapter.
- **Core sections** — with definitions cited to the academic literature and Malaysian regulatory documents.
- **Pedagogical boxes** throughout the text:
  - 📌 **Quick Note** — clarifies a tricky point in one or two sentences.
  - 💡 **Did You Know? (Trivia)** — surprising, verified facts to keep you curious.
  - 🧠 **Brainstormer** — open-ended questions to discuss in class or reflect on.
  - 🇲🇾 **Malaysian Spotlight** — a local institution, initiative or statistic.
- **Case Study** — a factual Malaysian case with discussion questions.
- **Chapter Summary** — key takeaways by section.
- **Key Terms** — definitions that feed into the end-of-book Glossary.
- **Test Your Knowledge** — 10 multiple-choice questions and 5 short-answer questions with an answer guide.
- **Problem-Solving Corner** — worked numerical and scenario problems.
- **References** — APA 7th edition.

**For lecturers:** each chapter maps to roughly one to two weeks of a 14-week semester. Chapters 1–3 suit the first half (foundations, banking and payments, digital financing); Chapters 4–7 suit the technology core (blockchain, trading and robo-advisory, crypto-assets, AI); Chapters 8–9 close with risk, regulation, ethics and Islamic/sustainable finance — a distinctive Malaysian strength.

**For practitioners:** the Case Studies, Malaysian Spotlights and Problem-Solving Corners are self-contained and can be read independently as professional briefings.

## Recommended Textbooks

### Main Recommended Textbook

**Pompella, M., & Matousek, R. (Eds.). (2021). *The Palgrave handbook of FinTech and blockchain*. Palgrave Macmillan.**

Recommended as the main companion text. Published within the last five years, this peer-reviewed handbook covers virtually every topic in this book — digital payments, crowdfunding, blockchain, cryptocurrencies, robo-advisory, RegTech and financial inclusion — with contributions from leading scholars. Its academic depth makes it ideal for students progressing to degree level, while individual chapters are readable enough for diploma students. Pair its conceptual chapters with the Malaysian applications presented in this book.

### Supplementary Textbooks

1. **Liaw, K. T. (Ed.). (2021). *The Routledge handbook of fintech*. Routledge.** — A comprehensive survey of FinTech development, funding and applications across banking, blockchain and capital markets; strong on the economics of FinTech business models (supports Chapters 1, 3 and 5).

2. **Gupta, P., & Tham, T. M. (2019). *Fintech: The new DNA of financial services*. De Gruyter.** — A structured, teaching-oriented treatment of FinTech segments with an Asian perspective; particularly useful for payments, P2P lending and robo-advisory (supports Chapters 1–3 and 5).

3. **Harvey, C. R., Ramachandran, A., & Santoro, J. (2021). *DeFi and the future of finance*. Wiley.** — A Duke University scholar's accessible yet rigorous introduction to decentralised finance; the best single short book for Chapters 4 and 6.

4. **Arslanian, H. (2022). *The book of crypto: The complete guide to understanding Bitcoin, cryptocurrencies and digital assets*. Palgrave Macmillan.** — A practitioner-scholar's guide to crypto-assets, tokenisation and institutional adoption (supports Chapter 6).

5. **Oseni, U. A., & Ali, S. N. (Eds.). (2019). *Fintech in Islamic finance: Theory and practice*. Routledge.** — The leading academic treatment of Islamic FinTech, essential for Chapter 9 and for understanding Malaysia's distinctive advantage.

6. **Chishti, S., Craddock, T., Courtneidge, R., & Zachariadis, M. (Eds.). (2020). *The PayTech book: The payment technology handbook for investors, entrepreneurs and FinTech visionaries*. Wiley.** — Practitioner chapters on payments infrastructure, QR and real-time payments (supports Chapter 2).

7. **King, B. (2021). *The rise of technosocialism: How inequality, AI and climate will usher in a new world order*. Marshall Cavendish Business.** — A big-picture, provocative read on where finance, AI and climate intersect; excellent for tutorial debates (supports Chapters 7–9).

In addition to these textbooks, students should treat the primary sources of **Bank Negara Malaysia** (e.g., the *Financial Sector Blueprint 2022–2026* and the *Licensing Framework for Digital Banks*), the **Securities Commission Malaysia** (e.g., the *Guidelines on Recognized Markets* and *Guidelines on Digital Assets*), and the **World Bank Global Findex Database** as required reading — they are cited throughout and are freely available online.
